Guglielmo Tommaso

* ‘ANOMALIE - specimen ∅’ - Laura EnricoMenswear collection, 2025

‘ANOMALIE - specimen ∅’ is a reflection on bodies not yet seen, or perhaps already forgotten: a fashion that is simultaneously alien and intimately human, born from a visceral need for protection in an uncertain future.

The concept draws from the aesthetic and philosophical universe of Luc Besson’s The Fifth Element, where science fiction becomes a distorting mirror used to question human nature, in a world dominated by dichotomies: good and evil, vulnerability and violence. The alien is not the “other”, but the device through which humanity observes its own imperfection. The collection inherits this vision: a hymn to life seeking redemption amidst chaos, celebrating a fluid, gender-neutral identity.

The influence of Jun Takahashi’s Undercover introduces the language of Chaos and Balance. The garments become dystopian armours that uniform and conceal the body, shielding it from a palpable existential unease. The sartorial vocabulary deforms and guards the figure, exposing it in unexpected ways.

To develop the collection, Laura explored the paradox of the technological age: a frenetic drive toward the unknown of outer space that serves as an escape from the planet Earth that humanity itself is dismantling. At the heart of this narrative move undefined beings, suspended between the human and the alien, post-human figures embodying the tension between the desire for discovery and the inevitable decay of their origins.

The silhouettes merge two seemingly distant worlds. A military aesthetic brings padding, patches and volumes that recall the functionality of uniforms, but also the fragility of post-war bandages, referencing the raw photographic reportage of Scott Nelson and Gianni Berengo Gardin. An aerospace aesthetic, with lunar textures, NASA space suits and 1960s sci-fi references, defines a “speculative uniform”.

The outfit features a double-zip jacket in a neutral ash blue, slightly oversized for a streetwear feel. White details run along the zip tapes, the stitching and the piping of the seams. The two zips change the jacket: closed from the top for structure, from the bottom for ease. Underneath, a loose jersey and fleece sweater made for layering. A nylon skirt plays with asymmetry and volume: its oval pockets can be closed for a gathered, bubbled effect, or left open to move.
