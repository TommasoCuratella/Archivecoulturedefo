* Martyna AgnieszkaDesigner

Martyna Agnieszka is a fashion designer based in Poland. For her, design is a way of telling a story through costume, and she addresses her clothes to people of a certain mindset rather than a specific age or physique.

Something out of nothing

Martyna has always had a sketchbook. Before fashion she took theatre and piano classes and recited poetry, and none of it became her real focus, but from those years she kept what she calls a life skill: knowing “how to create something out of nothing”. She built her own “oneiric world”, she says, and she is still living in it.

She has always seen fashion as a statement, “whether it was political or you just wanted to look cool”. That has not changed, in her words: “It just evolves.”

How she works

A project starts from what bothers her most, and from how she can look good in it. For Martyna a collection is above all an experience, a statement. She sews everything herself. She used to go through her patterns with a pattern maker; now she would rather work them out alone, and she refuses to use patterns that already exist. She is drawn to natural materials, to their texture and to “how alive it feels”.

“I think of what bothers me the most and how I can look good in it.”

By her own account she works in a chaotic way: a great deal of work in a short time, then months of rest, and again. Beyond practical skills, her studies taught her patience, and she finds that “the most interesting results arise when I live in peace with it”.

The first piece she felt was really hers was the first jacket she made, closed with two big buttons. It looked, she says, “like it was made for a human-sized porcelain doll”.

The Third World of Women

Her collection “The Third World of Women” is made of eight silhouettes, each standing for a woman who shaped her. Raw edges, virgin human hair and buttons moved to the right side, as on menswear, carry the idea of beauty as something built rather than displayed.

The idea took shape about two years ago, after she read Susan Sontag’s “On Women”, above all the essays “The Third World of Women” and “The Double Standard of Aging”, a book she thinks every girl and woman should read. The concern behind it is older. Why does aging enhance a man but progressively destroy a woman? Why was femininity always identified with incompetence?

How the eight women became eight silhouettes is in the article ‘The Third World of Women’.

Today

Martyna is now deciding “what is the next thing that bothers me. Or inspires, at least.” In the coming years she wants to keep educating herself and to show it through her work. One day she would like to hear someone say “I felt (…)”: to make an emotional impact, positive or negative.
